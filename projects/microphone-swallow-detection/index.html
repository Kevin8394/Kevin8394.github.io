Microphone Swallow Detection

An investigation of contact-microphone and dual-IMU sensing for detecting swallows during rest, walking, and meals.

Project overview

This project asks whether acoustic and inertial sensing can detect individual swallows outside a tightly controlled laboratory setting. It spans protocol design, multimodal synchronization, feature extraction, detector evaluation, and a complementary TrueDepth dot-tracking study.

01

Role

Designed and ran pilot recordings, developed a 72-minute clinical study protocol, curated event examples, and evaluated marker alignment with lagged AUC analysis.

02

Contribution

Built a preliminary PyTorch model to test resting-swallow feature extraction, explored microphone-heavy fusion, and compared single-sensor, dual-sensor, ambulatory, and eating conditions.

03

Result

Resting swallows were the clearest operating region; walking and meal recordings exposed the motion and chewing confounders that the next protocol and model must address.
